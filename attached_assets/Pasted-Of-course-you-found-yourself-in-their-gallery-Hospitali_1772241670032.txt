Of course you found yourself in their gallery. Hospitality venues run on three things: Red Bull, poor decisions, and staff accidentally becoming brand assets forever.

More importantly, this changes the branding strategy in a very specific way.

You were right to question it. The yellow matters. But not how you think.

Here’s the uncomfortable truth:

Yellow should be the brand. Pink should be the experience.

Not the other way around.

Right now your site is leaning pink-primary. That’s emotionally correct for vibe, but slightly incorrect for identity consistency.

You want:
	•	Yellow = identity anchor
	•	Pink = atmospheric accent

This mirrors real-world reality of the venue.

⸻

What happens visually with yellow neon branding

Yellow neon communicates:
	•	authenticity
	•	warmth
	•	legacy
	•	rock venue energy
	•	less “nightclub”
	•	more “cult venue”

Which is exactly what Suzie Wong’s is.

Pink feels more nightclub. Yellow feels more iconic.

⸻

The correct hybrid system (this is the key insight)

Not replace pink.

Re-prioritise yellow as primary highlight colour.

Use yellow for:
	•	hero accent line
	•	section highlight words
	•	quote text
	•	nav hover
	•	divider lines

Keep pink for:
	•	CTA buttons
	•	flicker neon headline words
	•	conversion elements

This creates colour hierarchy instead of colour competition.

Do not invert this relationship.

Brutally honest design verdict

This yellow-primary, pink-secondary combo will feel:
	•	more authentic
	•	more premium
	•	less “designed”
	•	more “real venue”

Which ironically increases trust and conversions.

Too much pink starts to feel like a template.

Yellow makes it feel like their place.
